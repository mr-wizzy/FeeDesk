# Feedesk: Refined Product Concept

## 1. Product summary

Feedesk is a free, mobile-first school fee tracking and payment portal
that connects a school's approved financial records to parents and
guardians. It helps families understand fees across multiple children,
pay through the school's configured payment gateway, verify payment and
receipt status, and review historical statements.

Feedesk uses the school's own records, protects private student
information, and directs unresolved questions to a named school staff
member rather than guessing.

**Business model:** Feedesk is free for schools and parents. The school
is the customer and controls its fee structure, student records, and
bank account. Parents and guardians use the portal at no charge.

**Important distinction:** Free software does not mean zero operating
cost. Payment gateway fees, hosting limits, maintenance, secure backups,
and support still need to be accounted for.

## 2. The problem

Community schools may keep fee information in spreadsheets and paper
ledgers. During term resumption, parents call or message the bursary to
ask what they owe, whether a transfer has been confirmed, or whether a
receipt has been issued.

Staff then have to locate transactions, match them to the right child,
update records, and respond to repeated questions. Similar student
names, missing references, and delayed updates can cause confusion and
loss of trust.

The central problem is not a lack of chat. It is the lack of a reliable,
visible link between the school's approved ledger and the information
parents can access.

## 3. Target users

### Parents and guardians

Parents and guardians are the primary users. Some use inexpensive
Android phones, have limited data, or are unfamiliar with apps. One
parent account can manage several children, but staff must approve each
parent-child relationship.

Parents need to know: - What each child owes for the current term. -
What has been paid and what remains outstanding. - Whether a payment is
initiated, confirmed, reconciled, and receipted. - What the payment and
clearance record was for a previous term. - Who to contact when a record
is missing or unresolved.

### School staff

Staff use a separate administration interface. They import and maintain
records, reconcile payments, correct errors, and link guardians to
students. Staff are not users of the parent-facing application.

### School owner or authorized approver

The owner or authorized approver approves published fee rules and
sensitive financial changes, reviews corrections, and oversees access
and data quality.

## 4. The five customer-facing features in version one

### Feature 1: My children's fees

**Parent asks:** "How much do I owe this term?"

**Feedesk answers:** Each child's fee items, charges, discounts,
payments, and outstanding balance, followed by the combined total.

**Record source:** The school's approved fee schedule and each child's
private ledger.

Each child is displayed separately. Every figure shows the relevant
term, date, and status. Totals are calculated from ledger entries using
fixed arithmetic, not generated by AI.

### Feature 2: Payment status and receipts

**Parent asks:** "Has my payment been confirmed?"

**Feedesk answers:** The amount, payment date, transaction reference,
status, and receipt reference if one has been issued.

**Record source:** Verified gateway transaction records and the school's
reconciled payment ledger.

An initiated payment is not a confirmed payment. A successful gateway
transaction is not automatically a school-issued receipt.

### Feature 3: Pay school fees

**Parent asks:** "How can I pay my outstanding fees?"

**Feedesk answers:** The approved amount due, supported payment methods,
any applicable gateway fee, and a secure way to start payment.

**Record source:** The approved ledger and payment gateway.

Parents can pay by card or bank transfer through the configured gateway.
Settlement goes to the school's own designated account. Feedesk records
payment progress and reconciles it with the school account.

### Feature 4: Previous term records

**Parent asks:** "What was my payment and clearance record for last
session's second term?"

**Feedesk answers:** The historical fee breakdown, payments,
adjustments, balances, receipts, and recorded clearance status for that
term.

**Record source:** The school's approved historical ledger.

If the historical paper ledger has not been digitized or verified,
Feedesk says that the record is unavailable. It must not reconstruct
history from estimates.

### Feature 5: Ask the school

**Parent asks:** A question Feedesk cannot safely answer, such as "Why
is this transfer still pending?"

**Feedesk answers:** What is known, what remains unresolved, and the
name of the authorized school staff member responsible for handling the
question. It provides an issue reference.

**Record source:** The relevant record status, approved school contact
details, and recorded escalation reason where available.

This is a handoff to a person, not an automated support agent. Version
one has no automated messages or follow-ups.

## 6. How records work

The school's existing spreadsheets and paper ledgers are the starting
point. Feedesk must not assume these records are ready for direct
import.

### Shared school records

Examples: - Fee rules and published fee schedules. - Term dates and
payment policies. - Approved school contact details.

Process: 1. Staff upload a spreadsheet or enter information through the
administration screen. 2. The information is checked for errors. 3. The
owner or authorized approver reviews and publishes it. 4. Each published
version has an effective date and records who approved it. 5. Parents
can search approved, published shared records by meaning.

Shared-record search is limited to approved information for the relevant
school.

### Private student records

Examples: - Individual charges and balances. - Payments and receipt
history. - Clearance and adjustment history.

Process: 1. Staff import or enter records from the school's ledger and
relevant payment sources. 2. Staff resolve duplicate students, missing
identifiers, and conflicting balances. 3. Authorized finance staff
verify financial entries. 4. Sensitive corrections receive a second
approval where possible. 5. Parents see only records for children they
are authorized to access.

**Privacy rule:** Private records must be retrieved using the
authenticated parent's exact customer ID, the exact student ID, and the
relevant school ID, with access checks on every request. Private records
must never be searched across customers. Hiding a record in the
interface is not sufficient; the server must enforce access
restrictions.

### Source-of-truth rules

-   The approved school ledger is the source of truth for what the
    school says is owed.
-   The payment gateway is the source of truth for the gateway
    transaction's status.
-   The school's reconciliation process determines how a received
    payment is applied to a student's account and when a school receipt
    is issued.

If these sources disagree, Feedesk flags the discrepancy, avoids
claiming that the account is cleared, and directs the parent to the
bursary.

Corrections must preserve an audit trail with the old value, new value,
reason, editor, approver, and time.

## 7. Payment process

Payment status and school receipt status are separate.

1.  **Payment initiated:** The parent starts a payment. Feedesk creates
    a unique payment reference and shows that the payment is in
    progress.
2.  **Gateway status checked:** Feedesk verifies the transaction using
    the gateway's server-side confirmation process. A return to the
    payment page alone is not proof of success.
3.  **School reconciliation:** The payment is matched to the right
    student and fee account. Bank transfers that cannot be matched
    remain unresolved.
4.  **Payment applied and receipted:** Once the required checks are
    complete, the ledger is updated and the school issues or records the
    receipt. Feedesk displays the receipt reference and relevant dates.

A payment can be successful at the gateway but still await school
reconciliation. The interface must show that distinction clearly.

For example: - Gateway status: Successful. - School reconciliation:
Pending. - School receipt: Not issued.

A half-finished or unresolved payment must never be shown as Paid.
Retrying a payment must not create duplicate charges or receipts.

## 8. Parent and student access

One parent can manage multiple children. Staff manage and approve the
parent-child relationships.

Before displaying private records, Feedesk verifies the authenticated
account and the approved relationship to each child. A child's name,
class, or admission number alone is not sufficient proof of identity.

Provide safe account recovery and a way to revoke lost or old sessions.
A sponsor must not see a child's complete financial history unless
properly authorized.

### When a child leaves the school

The user's decision is that only staff can access the records when a
child leaves.

-   Staff mark the student as withdrawn or inactive, with an effective
    date.
-   The student's previous fee statements, payment history, and receipts
    remain preserved for the school's recordkeeping obligations.
-   The former parent's access is revoked according to the school's
    authorized policy.
-   Authorized staff can retrieve historical records for legitimate
    administrative purposes.
-   The school defines retention and deletion periods consistent with
    its legal obligations.

Do not delete financial history merely because a child leaves, and do
not allow former guardians to retain access simply because they were
once linked to the student.

## 9. Day-one setup

Feedesk should launch with a school setup process, not an empty chat
screen.

Before parents rely on the portal, the school must: 1. Create the
school's account and configure its term. 2. Import current fee schedules
and student records. 3. Resolve duplicate students and missing
identifiers. 4. Match existing balances against the spreadsheet and
paper ledger. 5. Import and verify available historical records. 6.
Configure the payment gateway and the school's settlement account. 7.
Assign staff permissions and the named bursary contact. 8. Approve the
opening ledger and test parent access with sample accounts.

Parents must not see an invented balance, a blank answer presented as a
zero balance, or a payment button for an account that has not been
verified.

If the school has no approved records ready, Feedesk says setup is
incomplete and provides the named school contact.

## 10. What Feedesk must refuse to do

Feedesk must never: - Invent a balance or receipt when records are
missing. - Show one family's private records to another family. - Allow
a parent to change charges or clear a debt. - Claim that a gateway
transaction is a school-issued receipt. - Decide disputed payments,
grant fee waivers, or approve refunds on behalf of the school. - Promise
clearance when the official clearance record is missing or disputed. -
Search private records by meaning across multiple customers. - Send
automated reminders or perform background follow-ups in version one.

When a question cannot be answered safely, Feedesk explains the
limitation and directs the parent to the school's named staff member.
The fallback must not reveal internal notes, other parents' details, or
staff-only information.

## 11. Technology approach

Build Feedesk as a lightweight, mobile-first web application that works
on inexpensive Android phones and unreliable connections. Avoid large
downloads, video, and unnecessary screens. Make it easy to retry failed
requests.

A practical prototype can use: - HTML, CSS, and JavaScript for the
interface. - A small server-side application programming interface (API)
to check permissions and retrieve records. - A database with
authentication and access controls. - A payment gateway test environment
during development.

Free development tools and free service tiers may be enough for a
prototype with fictional data, but they do not guarantee unlimited
hosting, secure backups, or production reliability. Before handling real
student data or live payments, establish secure backups, recovery,
access controls, maintenance, and operating-cost plans.

### AI's role

AI must not calculate balances from raw transactions, decide whether a
payment is confirmed, or invent answers when records are missing.
Financial totals must use fixed arithmetic on approved ledger entries.

If meaning-based search is used for shared school rules, it must search
only approved and published shared records. Private financial records
must be fetched by exact authorized identifiers, never searched across
customers.

## 12. Free software model and payment costs

Feedesk is free for schools and parents. The school owns the financial
relationship with its families, and payments settle into the school's
designated bank account.

Free software still has operating costs. Payment gateway fees, hosting
limits, secure backups, maintenance, and support must be considered.
Possible future funding could include sponsorship, grants, institutional
partnerships, or a separately funded service arrangement. Do not place
essential student financial records behind a parent payment tier or
introduce advertisements into sensitive financial screens.

For a prototype, use free development tools and free service tiers with
understood limits. Confirm the current terms of any provider before
launch.

The payment gateway integration may be free to build and test, but live
transactions incur fees. Confirm the school's applicable merchant
pricing, tax treatment, settlement terms, and who bears each fee with
the gateway before launch. Feedesk should not pool school funds or act
as an intermediary that distributes them.

### Do not build yet

-   AI agents that take actions for parents.
-   Automated payment reminders or follow-up messages.
-   Automatic fee waivers, reversals, or changes to student balances.

## 14. How to measure success

Run a pilot with one school and manually verified records. Agree on the
measures before launch.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Measure                             Success definition
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Balance accuracy                    Every displayed balance matches the
                                      approved source ledger.

  Receipt accuracy                    No false claims that a payment has
                                      been receipted.

  Parent independence                 Parents can answer the three main
                                      fee questions without calling the
                                      bursary.

  Faster resolution                   Staff spend less time searching
                                      ledgers and answering routine
                                      payment questions.

  Data protection                     No unauthorized access to private
                                      student records.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Test the system against manually verified examples, including duplicate
student names, a failed payment, a successful but unreconciled transfer,
a missing historical record, and a parent linked to multiple children.

For financial accuracy and privacy, the target is zero known incorrect
confirmations and zero unauthorized disclosures. Any incident should
trigger investigation and correction.

## 15. Top three risks

### 1. Private records are exposed to the wrong parent

This can expose children's financial information, damage family trust,
and create disputes.

**Prevention:** Enforce access rules on the server for every
private-record request, verify guardian relationships, separate schools'
data, restrict administrative permissions, and test with unauthorized
accounts before launch.

### 2. A payment is reported incorrectly

A delayed gateway notification, duplicate transaction, unmatched
transfer, or stale ledger can cause a parent to pay twice or believe a
debt has been cleared when it has not.

**Prevention:** Verify gateway status, use unique transaction
references, make processing safe to retry, reconcile bank transfers, and
keep initiated, confirmed, receipted, failed, and disputed states
distinct.

### 3. Incorrect source records become trusted digital records

A spreadsheet may contain duplicate students, missing payments, or an
incorrect opening balance. A polished interface can make the error look
official.

**Prevention:** Validate imports, reconcile opening balances, require
approval before publication, preserve correction history, show when
records were last verified, and flag unresolved discrepancies. Do not
launch real financial claims until the school approves the initial data.

## 16. Recommended pilot plan

Start with one Lagos school, one term, and a verified opening ledger.

1.  Review the school's current spreadsheet and paper ledger formats
    using sample or fictional data.
2.  Define the data fields and unique identifiers needed for schools,
    students, guardians, terms, fee items, and transactions.
3.  Build the parent portal and separate administration screens.
4.  Test private-record access, fee calculations, historical statements,
    and all payment states with fictional data.
5.  Reconcile the school's opening balances and obtain approval before
    importing live records.
6.  Configure gateway test payments and verify settlement and
    reconciliation procedures.
7.  Run a controlled pilot, log issues, and measure the success
    criteria.
8.  Expand only after the school can trust the displayed balances and
    receipt statuses.

## Final product definition

Feedesk is a free, mobile-first school fee portal that connects a
school's approved financial records to parents and guardians. It helps
families understand fees across multiple children, pay through the
school's configured payment gateway, verify payment and receipt status,
and review historical statements. It protects private student
information, uses the school's own records, and directs unresolved
questions to a named school staff member rather than guessing.

**Open implementation decision:** For bank transfers, should Feedesk
generate a unique virtual account for each payment, or should parents
transfer to the school's existing bank account and the bursary match
payments manually? This choice affects how much payment reconciliation
can safely be automated in version one.
